Here are the key procedures and how they can help patients regain mobility and comfort: Total Knee Replacement (TKR): A procedure to replace the entire knee joint. It is typically recommended for severe knee arthritis (often osteoarthritis) where cartilage wear causes bone-on-bone pain​. In a TKR, the damaged portions of the femur (thigh bone), tibia (shin bone), and usually the underside of the kneecap are replaced with metal and medical-grade plastic components. The goal is to eliminate pain and stiffness, allowing you to walk and climb stairs without discomfort. At Paras Hospital, our knee replacements are performed with computer navigation guidance for optimal alignment, and patients are usually encouraged to start gentle movement the next day. Many people experience dramatic relief - knee replacement surgery has a success rate of about 90-95% in providing pain relief and improved knee function . Partial Knee Replacement (Unicondylar Knee Replacement): A procedure where only the damaged compartment of the knee joint is replaced, preserving healthy bone and ligaments. This is an option for patients whose arthritis is confined to one part of the knee. Because it's a smaller surgery than a total knee replacement, recovery can be faster and the knee may feel more "natural." Our surgeons will let you know if you're a candidate for a partial (unicompartmental) knee replacement or if a total replacement is more suitable. Total Hip Replacement (THR): In a total hip replacement, the ball and socket of the hip joint are both replaced with prosthetic components. The worn-out femoral head (ball) is replaced with a metal or ceramic ball on a stem, and the hip socket is lined with a metal shell and a durable plastic or ceramic liner​. THR is most often done for hip arthritis (osteoarthritis or rheumatoid arthritis causing hip pain and stiffness) or for serious hip joint damage (such as avascular necrosis or fractures that disrupt the joint). The surgery can relieve chronic groin/thigh pain, improve leg movement, and allow patients to return to activities like walking, driving, or even golf without pain. For some hip fractures (like a broken femoral neck in an older patient), a partial hip replacement may be performed, replacing only the ball of the joint with an implant​. Our hip replacement program emphasizes early mobilization - patients are usually assisted to stand and start walking with support within a day or two post-surgery. Shoulder Replacement: The shoulder is a ball-and-socket joint that can be severely affected by arthritis, rotator cuff injury arthritis, or trauma (like complex fractures). In a shoulder replacement, the damaged head of the humerus (upper arm bone) is replaced with a metal ball, and the socket (glenoid) may be resurfaced with a plastic component​. This procedure is recommended for patients with intense shoulder pain and loss of motion due to conditions such as osteoarthritis or rheumatoid arthritis in the shoulder, or for certain complex fractures where repair is not possible. After a shoulder replacement, patients often experience significant pain reduction and gradually regain the ability to raise and rotate their arm, making daily tasks easier (like combing hair or reaching overhead). Our surgeons perform total shoulder replacements as well as reverse shoulder replacements (a special technique used in cases of severe rotator cuff damage) depending on what will restore the best function for you. Other Joint Replacement Surgeries: Paras Hospital's expertise is not limited to knees, hips, and shoulders. We also offer: Elbow replacement for badly damaged elbows (often due to rheumatoid arthritis or post-traumatic arthritis), where the elbow joint is replaced with a hinged artificial joint​; Ankle replacement for advanced ankle arthritis, to relieve pain and maintain ankle movement​; and small joint replacements (like for finger joints in severe rheumatoid arthritis) to improve hand function. Each of these procedures is performed by surgeons experienced in that specific joint, ensuring specialized care.
